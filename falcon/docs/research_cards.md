# 100 candidate research cards (generated 2026-08-22)

Produced by a 10-lens fan-out; each lens proposed 10 directions with a

## 1. round breaks SamplerZ: required acceptance probability exceeds 1 at a quarter of the leaves
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: 2024/1709 §7.1's floor→round swap is not a drop-in. It makes r = mu − round(mu) land in [−1/2,1/2), violating the precondition x >= 0 that the standardized isochronous SamplerZ is built on (cref/sign.c:1302 'Since x >= 0, we can use fpr_trunc()'; cref/fpr.h:889 'the result will fit, since x >= 0 and ccs < 1'). For (b=0, z0=1, r <~ −0.22) the required acceptance ccs*exp(−x) exceeds 1 at every leaf with sigma' < 1.3932, so the rejection sampler is invalid and the sampled distribution is biased by ~1e-1 against a spec budget of 2^-46. A preliminary run over three real FALCON-512 keys already puts 26–29% of the 512 leaves below that threshold with peak required acceptance 1.09–1.16. Restoring validity needs sigma_max >= 2*sigma_min ≈ 2.556 instead of 1.8205, i.e. a new RCDT table, ~40% more iterations and PRNG bytes per draw, a new isochrony proof and new KATs.
- experiment: (a) Scale the margin computation in scratchpad/leafsig.jl to 1000 keys at n=512 and n=1024: report the fraction of leaves with margin < 0, the peak required acceptance, and the analytic threshold sigma*. (b) Add a round-based SamplerZ to src/samplerz.jl behind a Ref flag (same pattern as FFSAMPLING_CREF/CDIV_CREF) and chi-square the sampled z against the exact D_{Z,r,sigma'} over 1e7 draws at a sigma'=1.28 leaf, sweeping r; expect rejection at overwhelming significance for r < −0.22. (c) Build the C reference with FALCON_AVX2=1 and feed fpr_expm_p63 an r<0: show (int64_t)(y*2^63) with y>1 saturating to INT64_MIN, so z becomes all-ones and the sampler accepts unconditionally. (d) Solve numerically for the minimal sigma_max restoring validity for all leaves and price the resulting base sampler (RCDT entries, expected iterations, bytes per draw).
- kill: The margin is >= 0 for every leaf over 1000 keys at both degrees (my analytic threshold sigma* = 1.3932 is wrong), or 2024/1709 §7.1 in fact specifies a widened / symmetric base proposal alongside the round, so 'replace floor by round' was never proposed as a drop-in on the existing Algorithm 15 — in which case there is no break, only a restatement of their own construction.

## 2. A round-based FALCON leaks the Gram-Schmidt norms in its plain signatures
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: The §7.1 bias of idea 1 is not uniform across the tree: its magnitude at leaf i is a monotone function of sigma' = sigma/||b~_i||, so a round-based signer's output distribution depends on the secret basis's Gram-Schmidt profile — exactly the quantity Fouque et al. (EUROCRYPT 2020) recover to break FALCON, and which they need a physical side channel to see. If the bias survives into the published s2, the countermeasure converts a 7.5e-6 two-implementation oracle into a single-implementation, no-side-channel, statistical key-recovery attack in the style of learning-a-parallelepiped.
- experiment: Sign N = 1e5 then 1e6 messages on the same 20 keys with floor-FALCON and with round-FALCON (the flag from idea 1). Compute the empirical second and fourth moments of the coefficient vectors of (s1,s2) and of s2 alone. Test whether the round covariance deviates from the ideal isotropic q*sigma^2*I, and regress the deviation on the known per-leaf ||b~_i|| (which we hold, from expand_privkey) to see whether the deviation direction tracks BB*. If a per-leaf estimator emerges, estimate all 512 GS norms from signatures alone and feed them into the published GS-norm recovery.
- kill: At 1e6 signatures the round-signature covariance is statistically indistinguishable from the floor-signature covariance (Frobenius deviation inside the Monte-Carlo band), or the deviation exists but is uncorrelated with the true GS norms — i.e. the per-leaf bias averages out over the uniform r and never reaches the transmitted s2. Either outcome kills the attack while leaving idea 1's correctness break intact.

## 3. Odd ||(g,-f)|| squared is unreachable by the standardized key generation, and the reason is the solver
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: The even-norm idiosyncrasy that blocks §7.1 is not a quirk of the C reference, it is a load-bearing requirement of the standardized NTRU solver. poly_small_mkgauss (cref/keygen.c:4118-4131) forces sum-of-coefficients ≡ 1 mod 2 for BOTH f and g, because zint_bezout (cref/keygen.c:1569) states 'The two integers must be odd'; and ||f||^2+||g||^2 ≡ f(1)+g(1) mod 2, so both-odd forces the norm even. §7.1's odd-norm condition therefore forces exactly one resultant even, which the reference's constant-time binary extended GCD cannot process at all. Our Julia solver uses a generic Euclidean xgcd (src/ntrugen.jl:403 xgcd_floor), which means this implementation may be the only one that can build §7.1-conformant keys today.
- experiment: Patch ntru_gen in src/ntrugen.jl to force f(1) odd and g(1) even (and the mirror case), generate 1000 keys, and measure: (i) ntru_solve success rate and wall time versus standard parity, (ii) the ||(g,-f)||^2 and gs_norm distributions and the overall keygen rejection rate, (iii) whether (F,G) still fit MAX_FG_BITS so the key remains encodable, (iv) whether h = g/f mod q still exists at the same rate. Then attempt a constant-time patch to zint_bezout that strips the 2-adic valuation of the even input, and count the extra branches/lines and the cycle cost.
- kill: Odd-parity keys solve at the same rate with the same key statistics AND a <=20-line constant-time zint_bezout patch handles one even input with no measurable cost — then the obstruction is pure engineering, §7.1's key-generation side is cheap to satisfy, and this becomes a FIPS 206 footnote rather than a hole.

## 4. Bit-exact KATs are blind to this bug at 100 vectors; stress vectors that are not
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: low | cost: hours
- hypothesis: FIPS 206's headline conformance mechanism cannot detect the class it is meant to police. At the measured ~1e-5 divergence rate, a KAT set of O(100) random vectors passes a non-conforming implementation with probability 0.999; detecting A2 at 95% confidence needs ~4e5 random vectors. But the sensitive centres are searchable: for a fixed KAT key an exact-integer centre occurs at calls 1,2 with probability 1/q = 8.14e-5 (~12k message tries) and at calls 2n-1,2n with probability 1/||(g,-f)||^2 = 6.08e-5. A published vector that sits exactly on such a centre forces any respelled implementation to take the other side of floor deterministically, so a dozen adversarial 'stress vectors' replace a 4e5-vector statistical test with a 12-vector deterministic one. This is a constructive fix for CAVP/ACVP, not just a criticism.
- experiment: Extend scripts/heuristic1_check.jl to search ~1e6 (message, salt) pairs against one fixed key for centres with mu exactly integral (and separately |mu - round(mu)| < 1e-15) at each of the four positions, exploiting that calls 1,2 depend on c alone and calls 2n-1,2n on the PRNG. For each hit, sign under each combination of FFSAMPLING_CREF / CDIV_CREF / LDL_CREF and record the empirical conditional divergence probability. Report how many vectors give >= 0.99 detection for A1, A2 and for an FMA build, and publish the vector format (key, message, salt, seed, expected signature). Build the half-integer analogue for testing round-based implementations.
- kill: The conditional divergence probability given an exact-integer centre is below ~1e-3, so a hit does not reliably separate two spellings and no feasible number of stress vectors detects anything; or no exact-integer centre is found in 1e6 tries because the Float64 centre never lands exactly on an integer (only near it), removing the determinism the construction depends on.

## 5. The key-recovery window is the last six sampler calls, not the last two
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: A divergence is exploitable only when few calls follow it: after a divergence the PRNG desynchronizes, so a divergence at leaf 2n-1-k leaves Delta z0 supported on at most k+1 coefficients. The §5.1 recovery is then a search over [-19,19]^{k+1}: trivial for k <= 3 (~2.4e6) and meet-in-the-middle feasible for k <= 5. Both 2024/1709 §5.1 and our own §6.1 stop at k=1. If recovery works at k=3, then §7.1's odd-norm argument — which is entirely about the last two calls' denominator ||(g,-f)||^2 — leaves the 3rd through 6th from last calls unprotected while they remain full key-recovery positions. This is a claim about which positions are exploitable if they diverge, not about how often they diverge, so it does not re-open the already-refuted random-message interior-rate question.
- experiment: (i) Generalize scripts/key_recovery.jl from Delta z0 = a + b x^{n/2} to a support-<=4 Delta z0 over the coefficients that leaves 2n-4..2n-1 occupy under the tree's bit-reversed order. (ii) Force a divergence by injecting a +-1 change at leaf 2n-4 in one of two otherwise identical runs (the injection hook in scripts/precision_law.jl already targets a chosen leaf), then run the recovery from (s, s') and the public h alone. (iii) Repeat at 2n-6 with a meet-in-the-middle search. (iv) Combine with idea 6's denominators to get the natural per-position divergence rate at those leaves.
- kill: Recovery fails on a forced 2n-4 divergence — because the desynchronized tail drives Delta z0's coefficients outside the [-19,19] box, or because Delta s is not of the form Delta z0 * (g,-f) once more than the terminal leaves move (the merge itself contributes). Then the window really is two wide and §7.1's odd-norm argument covers all of it.

## 6. Exact denominators of all 2n centres: a characterisation of where round can protect
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: Every leaf centre is an exact rational p_j/d_j, and a round-based countermeasure protects leaf j if and only if d_j is odd — a half-integer centre requires an even denominator exactly as an integer centre requires the numerator to vanish. Heuristic 1's d = q and d = ||(g,-f)||^2 are heuristics (2024/1709's own Remark 1 says they cannot be made theorems). If the true last-two denominator carries a factor of 2 that is independent of the norm — introduced by the LDL division or the splitting — then forcing ||(g,-f)||^2 odd does not remove half-integer centres and §7.1 fails outright at the very position it targets.
- experiment: Run ffSampling a second time over Rational{BigInt} in exact arithmetic, in lockstep with the Float64 run (feeding it the same sampled integers), at n = 64, 128 and 256 first and a handful of signatures at n = 512 if numerators stay tractable. For each of the 2n leaves record the exact denominator, its 2-adic valuation and its odd part; test the predicted forms q, ||(g,-f)||^2 and q^k; and derive the general recursion for d_j from the l10 correction. Deliverable: the denominator atlas plus the 'odd denominator iff round protects' characterisation, and the exact-arithmetic ground truth idea 10 needs.
- kill: The exact rational computation blows up (numerators past a few 1e5 digits already at n=128) so no atlas is obtainable at any usable degree; or every d_j turns out odd once ||(g,-f)||^2 is odd, in which case §7.1 is sound on this axis and the result is a confirmation of their heuristic rather than a break of it.

## 7. The reference's own AVX2 build already violates the FIPS 206 plan, and the price of complying
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: FIPS 206 is expected to forbid FMA and fix the FP operation order, but the NIST-submitted reference's AVX2 path calls _mm256_fmadd_pd explicitly in the FFT butterflies, poly_mul_fft, poly_invnorm2_fft and poly_LDL_fft's mu/D11 (cref/fft.c:1057-1067 — the exact computation of our Class I(b)), and ships a different ALGORITHM for the acceptance probability: fpr_expm_p63 is a 64-bit integer fixed-point Horner in the FPEMU build (cref/fpr.c:1302), a double-precision Horner in FPNATIVE scalar (cref/fpr.h:899) and a double-precision tree with FMA in FPNATIVE AVX2 (cref/fpr.h:868-881). Three official builds of one submission that cannot agree bit-for-bit, selected by liboqs/PQClean at build or dispatch time. So the mandate invalidates the reference's own fast code, and 'fix the order of floating-point operations' does not even describe the FPEMU acceptance path, which is not floating point at all.
- experiment: Build the bundled cref three ways — scalar FPNATIVE, AVX2 with FMADD = _mm256_fmadd_pd, and AVX2 with inner.h:111's already-present mul+add fallback macros — on this machine (avx2 and fma are present in /proc/cpuinfo). Sign identical (key, message, seed) triples and (a) measure pairwise divergence rates over ~1e6 signatures, using the third build to separate 'vectorization' from 'FMA'; (b) benchmark sign and keygen for the no-FMA AVX2 build against the FMA one, pricing the mandate; (c) evaluate all three fpr_expm_p63 variants on 1e8 shared (r, ccs) inputs, histogram the 64-bit output differences, and convert to a per-signature accept/reject flip rate.
- kill: Scalar and AVX2 signatures agree over 1e6 signatures, so nothing deployment-specific remains beyond 2024/1709 §6.2's FMA result; or the no-FMA AVX2 build costs under 2% at signing, so the mandate is essentially free and there is no trade-off to report.

## 8. round makes the rejection loop's iteration count depend on the secret centre
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: The isochrony guarantee the specification inherits from Howe-Prest-Ricosset-Rossi rests on the acceptance being ccs*exp(-x) with x >= 0, which keeps the expected iteration count a smooth, centre-independent quantity. Under §7.1's round, at every leaf with sigma' < 1.3932 the acceptance saturates at 1 for r below a sigma'-dependent threshold (~-0.22 at sigma_min), so E[iterations | r] acquires a kink as a function of the secret centre. The countermeasure therefore breaks the hypothesis of the isochrony proof it inherits — trading a determinism bug for a timing-model violation. This is a statement about the proof's hypothesis and an exact counting quantity, not the already-dismissed question of whether BerExp byte consumption is externally observable.
- experiment: Instrument samplerz/samplerz_isigma to return their loop-iteration count (an exact integer, no timing involved). Sweep r over [-1/2,1/2) in 1000 bins x 1e5 draws at leaf widths 1.28, 1.35, 1.45 and 1.75, under floor and under round, and plot E[iterations | r] and the full iteration-count distribution. Bound the Renyi divergence of the running-time distribution across r and compare against the isochrony bound the specification cites. Only then ask, with scripts/dudect.jl, whether the kink survives into wall-clock on this machine.
- kill: E[iterations | r] is flat under round to within Monte-Carlo error at 1e5 draws per bin — the clamp is too rare to move the mean — or the kink exists but is smaller than the r-dependence that floor-based SamplerZ already has and that the existing isochrony bound already absorbs.

## 9. 'Fixed operation order' is not a specifiable predicate; the normative object is an FP dataflow graph
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: FIPS 206's stated plan to fix the order of floating-point operations cannot be written as normative text that determines the bytes, because our A2 result is a difference of operation SET, not order: 1/(br^2+bi^2) then two multiplies versus Smith's algorithm, and three multiplies versus one for D11 — both already 'in order'. The only object that pins the output is the straight-line dataflow graph of FP operations for one signature. Building it and a checker for it is simultaneously the right normative fix and a general tool: the first divergent node in a DAG diff IS the underdetermination, for any floating-point cryptographic implementation.
- experiment: Add a trace mode to the Julia FP primitives in src/fft.jl and src/ffsampling.jl emitting (op, operand bit patterns, result bit pattern) in execution order; do the same for the C reference through a shim around fpr.h's inline functions (scripts/cref_shim.c is the existing template). Sign one identical (key, message, seed) triple in both, diff the traces, and confirm the first divergent node is the complex division / D11 / bottom-level block already known. Then measure trace size per signature, diff time, and whether the tool independently rediscovers A1, A2 and the AVX2/FMA difference with no prior knowledge; and state how large a normative DAG for FALCON-512 would be.
- kill: The per-signature trace exceeds ~1e7 nodes or varies between builds enough that benign reassociation dominates the diff; or the first divergent node is uninformative (a deep FFT butterfly rather than the responsible formula), so the tool localizes nothing a human reading the two sources did not already find.

## 10. Denominator snapping: a countermeasure that dominates 2024/1709 section 7.1
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: At the sensitive positions the centre is p/d with d a known per-key integer (q at calls 1,2; ||(g,-f)||^2 ~ 1.6e4 at calls 2n-1,2n), and the Float64 centre is accurate to ~1e-13, so d * error ~ 2e-9, far below 1/2. Hence p = round(mu_fp * d) recovers the exact numerator and floor(mu) = fld(p, d) exactly. A three-line integer patch at 12 of the 2n leaves makes those leaves bit-deterministic across every conforming implementation, with no key-generation change, no parity condition, no change to the sampled distribution (it computes the mathematically correct centre), no new RCDT and no isochrony change — dominating §7.1 on every axis attacked in ideas 1, 2, 3 and 8. Its reach is exactly the in-precision window: it is sound while d * (FP error) << 1/2, i.e. d <~ 1e12, which is the first and last six calls, and outside that window integer centres cannot occur anyway. That coincidence is itself the theorem.
- experiment: Implement snapping in src/ffsampling.jl at the first six and last six leaves using d = q, d = ||(g,-f)||^2, and idea 6's d_j for the rest. Verify over 1e5 signatures that (i) round(mu_fp * d)/d equals the exact rational centre from idea 6 in every case, (ii) the A1 and A2 arms give zero divergences at the snapped positions over 4e5 signatures via scripts/divergence_accum.jl, (iii) the sampled z distribution is unchanged (chi-square against the unpatched run), (iv) the added cost in ms per signature. Then sweep synthetic d upward to locate the break-even denominator where snapping stops being sound.
- kill: The snapped centre disagrees with the exact rational centre of idea 6 at a rate comparable to 1e-5 — meaning the FP centre is not reliably within 1/(2d) of the truth, so snapping would itself become a new divergence source rather than removing one; or ||(g,-f)||^2 cannot be used because the integer division by a secret d is a new timing channel with no constant-time realisation at acceptable cost.

## 11. Hedged signing reintroduces the same-syndrome condition the attack needs
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: The FP key-recovery attack requires the same syndrome signed twice; plain randomized Falcon prevents this because a fresh 40-byte salt makes c = H(salt||msg) fresh every time. But hedged signing (derive both salt and sampler bytes from a PRF of sk and msg, the construction being pushed for embedded/HSM deployments to survive RNG failure) makes the *entire* transcript deterministic per (sk,msg). Two cross-vendor hedged builds signing the same message therefore hit an identical salt, identical syndrome, and identical sampler byte stream, differing only in FP spelling -- exactly the A2 condition -- so the RNG-failure countermeasure is precisely what re-enables the attack, and it does so in the deployments most likely to run heterogeneous builds.
- experiment: In falcon.jl replace the randombytes closure with a hedged source rb = chacha20(shake256(sk_seed || msg, 56)) so salt and sampler bytes are PRF-derived and deterministic per (sk,msg). Reuse the divergence_accum.jl / first_divergence.jl harness: sign the same message twice, once with the C spelling and once with with_spec_spelling(cdiv=true, ldl=true), and measure the divergence rate and the sampler position of each divergence. On any last-two divergence, run key_recovery.jl. Also vary the hedge input (msg only vs msg+counter) to confirm the centres still land on the n_k/q and n_k/||(g,-f)||^2 rationals.
- kill: If under hedged signing the divergence rate over ~4e5 signatures is statistically indistinguishable from zero, or the near-integer-centre structure disappears (e.g. hedging perturbs the centre off the q / ||(g,-f)||^2 rationals so |g*c - round(g*c)| is no longer ~1e-15), then hedging does not create exploitable same-syndrome pairs and the idea is dead.

## 12. Accumulating first-two-call divergences into key recovery where 2024/1709 said it is infeasible
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: 2024/1709 recovers the whole key from ONE last-two divergence but dismisses first-two divergences as yielding only 'a short lattice vector... not expected to be short enough to make key recovery feasible' (their section 5). In a derandomized/IBE/chosen-message setting an adversary can collect MANY first-two divergent pairs (rate ~1/q = 8.1e-5 per pair, independent across chosen messages for a fixed key). We hypothesize that K such short vectors together span enough of the secret lattice that LLL/BKZ recovers (f,g), overturning the infeasibility claim.
- experiment: Synthesize genuine first-two divergences cheaply: use the precision_law.jl injection hook but gate ON to positions 0,1 only, producing real Delta-s vectors from first-two straddles for a fixed key over many messages. Record each Delta-s and its exact algebraic form (Delta-z1 = a' + b'x^{n/2} times the top basis row). First measure the vector norms and compare to the Gaussian heuristic / the key norm ~sqrt(16000)~126 to see if accumulation can ever reach the key; then run LLL/BKZ (feasible dimension on 4 cores) on the sublattice spanned by K vectors plus the q-ary lattice and check whether (f,g) falls out as K grows.
- kill: If the first-two Delta-s vectors are rank-deficient (all integer multiples of one public vector) or lie in a coset whose provable minimum stays above what BKZ can reach even for K = n independent vectors, so no reduction yields (f,g), then 2024/1709's infeasibility claim stands and the idea is dead.

## 13. Deterministic IBE key derivation leaks the master trapdoor across heterogeneous PKGs
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: In GPV/DLP-style IBE built on Falcon's trapdoor the extracted user key IS a Falcon preimage of hash(identity), and stateless PKGs derive it deterministically (so a user can re-derive their key). Two PKG deployments or a PKG migrated across builds extracting the SAME identity produce an A2 pair; a last-two divergence recovers, via 2024/1709 section 5.1, not a single signing key but the MASTER trapdoor (f,g). Because the adversary registers identities, it controls the input and can request many extractions to reach the 1/||(g,-f)||^2 exposure.
- experiment: Model IBE extract as a derandomized sample_preimage of hash_to_point(identity) under the master key, over many adversary-chosen identities, once with the C spelling and once with with_spec_spelling. Reuse divergence_accum.jl to find a last-two divergent identity, then run key_recovery.jl to confirm the recovered (f,g) is the master key (it reproduces h) rather than a per-user key. Quantify expected #identities to recovery as 1/rate.
- kill: If realistic IBE extraction is necessarily randomized for security (fresh GPV randomness per extraction) so the same identity never yields two identical syndromes, and no deployed stateless/deterministic variant exists, then there is no pair to exploit and the idea is dead.

## 14. Rounding-mode, FTZ/DAZ, and precision-mode differences as divergence amplifiers
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: 2024/1709 studies FMA and dyn/tree. Cross-platform FP *configuration* differences (round-to-nearest vs round-toward-zero, flush-to-zero/denormals-are-zero, x87 80-bit vs SSE2 64-bit intermediates) are a distinct, realistic interop hazard that perturbs the centre with a DIRECTIONAL bias rather than the symmetric ~1e-14 of a spelling difference, so at the structured centres they may straddle the floor far more often than 1e-5 -- turning a mere misconfiguration into a much stronger key-recovery oracle.
- experiment: Two-pronged. (1) Runnable now in Julia: extend precision_law.jl to inject a DIRECTIONAL perturbation (always toward zero / always positive) of the same magnitude as the uniform one already tested, and compare divergence rate -- does bias direction beat symmetric noise at the exact-integer centres? (2) Realism via the existing C build path: build the C reference with a fesetround(FE_TOWARDZERO) shim (and separately with FTZ/DAZ set) in cref_shim.c, sign the same syndrome as the normal build, and count divergences and their positions. On any last-two divergence run key_recovery.
- kill: If directional perturbation and the fesetround/FTZ C builds give a divergence rate no higher than the ~1e-5 spelling baseline (or 0, i.e. they only move the width like arm B), then these config differences add no amplification and fold into existing results -- dead as a new vector.

## 15. Temporal build heterogeneity: cataloguing the minimal code deltas a library update must not touch
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: A within-vendor library update (a bug fix touching the FFT/LDL/complex-division code) is the same 'two spellings' condition displaced in time: under hedged/derandomized signing, an attacker who logged pre-update signatures and triggers a post-update re-sign of the same message gets an A2 pair. We hypothesize the set of signature-affecting single-line changes is small and enumerable, giving a concrete 'do-not-touch' list for FIPS-206-conformant maintenance -- and that some plausibly-real one-line refactors (Smith's complex division, 3-mul vs 1-mul D11, folded 1/sqrt(2) constants) are already known to be in it.
- experiment: Systematically enumerate minimal deltas over the FP primitives (div_fft, mul_fft, the LDL D11 spelling, the logn==2 unrolled leaves, associativity reorderings in add/sub/merge/split) using the with_spec_spelling toggles plus a few hand-made one-line variants, and for each measure whether it ever flips a signature (reuse divergence_accum.jl) and at which positions. Produce a table: delta -> reaches-signature? -> dangerous-position? Cross-check against the project's own A1/A2 classification.
- kill: If every enumerated single-line delta either always changes the signature (so 'reaches output' is not selective and the catalogue is trivial) or never does outside the already-known A1/A2 set (so there is nothing new to catalogue), the exercise yields no actionable maintenance guidance and is dead.

## 16. Expanded-key stored-tree vs rebuilt-tree interop as a standardized-format A2 trigger
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: Falcon's expanded private key stores the precomputed LDL tree for fast signing, and the LDL D11 entry (A2, class I-b) lives inside that tree. A deployment that SHIPS an expanded key built by implementation X, then signs on implementation Y that instead REBUILDS the tree from the raw (f,g) with a different spelling, diverges at the A2 rate on the same syndrome -- while arm B (fpr_inv_sigma width) provably does not reach the signature. Thus the standardized expanded-key format makes the tree-building spelling normative, and 'store vs rebuild' is a real interop axis distinct from the signing-mode axis 2024/1709 studied.
- experiment: Build the tree once with the C spelling and once with with_spec_spelling(ldl=true, cdiv=true) (respelled_tree in key_recovery.jl already does this), attach each to the same raw key, sign the same syndrome from both, and measure divergence rate and positions with divergence_accum.jl; run key_recovery on last-two hits. Separately confirm that a width-only tree difference (arm B) yields 0, isolating D11 as the load-bearing tree entry. Frame the result as: FIPS-206 expanded-key KATs must fix the tree-building spelling, not only the signing order.
- kill: If store-vs-rebuild divergence over ~4e5 signatures is indistinguishable from the already-measured A2 rate AND never lands at the last two calls (so no recovery), the expanded-key framing adds nothing beyond the known A2 result and is dead.

## 17. VM snapshot/restore and live-migration replay make even randomized Falcon exploitable
- impact: **incremental** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: Randomized Falcon is normally safe because salt+randomness are fresh each signature. But checkpoint/restore (CRIU), VM live migration, or crash-recovery can replay the exact RNG state AFTER the salt and sampler bytes are fixed; if signing then resumes on a migrated host with a different build/FP config, the replayed signature shares the salt and syndrome but differs in spelling -- an A2 pair -- with NO derandomization required. The exploitability hinges on where a restorable checkpoint can sit in the reference's control flow (salt drawn once in falcon_sign_start, sampler bytes streamed thereafter).
- experiment: Analyze the reference control flow to locate a checkpoint boundary that captures the drawn salt and the sampler PRNG state before ffSampling consumes them (cref_shim.c already seeds the sampler state directly, proving this state is snapshot-restorable). Then demonstrate: capture (salt, 56-byte sampler state) from one signature and replay it into the other spelling (first_divergence.jl already does exactly this seeding), measuring the resulting divergence rate as the 'snapshot-then-migrate' rate; on last-two hits run key_recovery.

## 21. The first-two divergence is not a vector — it is a linear equation on f mod q

## 23. Directed rounding turns the 1e-6 leak into a 1.6e-4 leak: a practical-cost key recovery
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours

## 32. Lemma 3: the acceptance threshold is SamplerZ's third real input, and it is Lipschitz
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours

Nobody has analysed the third. It is continuous, so its contribution is a Lipschitz bound rather than a straddle: an exp implementation with relative error e flips one acceptance with probability ~e, giving rate <= 2n*k*e with k the mean rejection-loop count. At the specification's ApproxExp accuracy (~2^-50) that is ~1e-12 and harmless -- but it degrades linearly, so a table-based or float32 exp (e ~ 2^-20) gives ~1e-3. Establishing this completes the characterisation: floor(mu) is the only non-Lipschitz point in the entire signer, which is exactly the 'theorem that says when an underdetermination reaches the output' the project is missing.
- experiment: Instrument samplerz.jl/berexp to record, per call, the comparison margin between the drawn 64-bit value and z, plus the rejection-loop count, over ~1e6 calls; this gives the empirical density of the margin near zero and hence the exact constant in rate = 2n*k*e. Validate the law by injecting a controlled relative perturbation e into the expm result at e = 2^-20, 2^-25, 2^-30, 2^-35 and measuring the end-to-end signature divergence rate (at 2^-20 the rate should be ~1e-3, so ~1e4 signatures per point suffices); check the measured points fall on the predicted line. Then audit every remaining discontinuous construct in the signing path (fpr_floor, fpr_trunc in BerExp's s, the gaussian0 CDT comparison, the int64 cast in expm) and show each is either exact-integer or covered.
- kill: Dead as a clean lemma if the measured divergence rate under injected exp error e is superlinear in e or exceeds the 2n*k*e bound by more than 3x (that would mean an acceptance flip cascades into further flips rather than just desynchronising, i.e. the bound is not compositional). Also dead if BerExp's fpr_trunc of x*inv_log2 turns out to be a *reachable* discontinuity that changes z by more than 1 ulp, since then the third input is not Lipschitz after all.

## 33. More precision makes it worse: the leak rate has a nonzero limit as precision goes to infinity
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: At an exact-integer centre the divergence probability between two implementations is P(their rounding errors have opposite signs), which does not vanish as precision improves. Section 6.3/#062 showed the two respellings share most of their rounding offset, so P ~ |dmu|/spread ~ 0.1; but two implementations at *different* precision share nothing, so P -> 1/2 and the rate saturates the Heuristic-1 bound 2/q + 2/||(g,-f)||^2 ~ 2.9e-4. Prediction: a 113-bit (or double-double) tree disagrees with the double reference at ~1.4e-4 per signature -- about 20x MORE often than the two double spellings disagree with each other -- and, unlike A2, the first two calls diverge too. This kills the most natural implementer's mitigation ('compute the tree in higher precision to be safe') and it is the missing normative point for the specification's silence on FLT_EVAL_METHOD / x87 precision control, which the reference guards with set_fpu_cw(2) and the specification never mentions.
- experiment: Run the tree construction (ffldl/normalize) and ffsampling_fft in BigFloat at 113 bits -- built into Julia via MPFR, no package install needed, or a hand-rolled Dekker/Knuth double-double if BigFloat is too slow -- rounding only the final centre to Float64 before samplerz; compare against the plain Float64 run on identical key, message and ChaCha20 state, over ~1e5 signatures (expect ~14 events at the predicted rate, enough for a factor-2 estimate). Record the position of each divergence and test the prediction that both ends fire, unlike A2. Cross-check the reverse direction with a mantissa-truncation hook at p = 45 and 40 bits, where the same argument predicts the *same* structured-position rate.
- kill: Dead if the 113-bit-vs-double rate comes out at or below the A2/A1 rate (<= 2e-5 over 1e5 signatures), because then higher precision does share the rounding offset and the saturation-at-1/2 model is wrong. Also dead if the divergences remain confined to the last two calls, since that would mean the first-two shared-offset suppression is precision-independent rather than a shared-rounding artefact.

## 34. The FP environment is a co-resident adversary: MXCSR rounding mode and FTZ/DAZ
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: low | cost: hours
- hypothesis: The signer's rounding mode is process-global mutable state that FALCON never sets: set_fpu_cw() is a no-op outside 32-bit x86, and nothing in falcon.c or sign.c touches MXCSR's RC or FTZ/DAZ bits. Anything else in the address space can change them -- another library, a plugin, a JIT, or simply linking the application with -Ofast, which pulls in crtfastmath.o and sets FTZ/DAZ process-wide. Under directed rounding (FE_UPWARD / FE_DOWNWARD / FE_TOWARDZERO) every centre acquires a *systematically signed* error, so at an exact-integer centre the floor lands on a predictable side and P(straddle vs an RNE signer) approaches 1, saturating the 2.9e-4 bound -- 40x the spelling rate, and adversarially triggerable rather than accidental. This is a new adversary model: not a second implementation, but a co-resident library. Note also that an FPEMU build ignores the hardware rounding mode entirely, so an FPEMU signer and a native signer in the same perturbed process diverge by construction.
- experiment: Wrap the shim's sign call in fesetround(FE_UPWARD/FE_DOWNWARD/FE_TOWARDZERO) and in _MM_SET_FLUSH_ZERO_MODE(_MM_FLUSH_ZERO_ON), and compare against FE_TONEAREST on identical key/message/ChaCha20 state over ~1e5 signatures per mode; measure the rate, the position histogram (predict both ends, with a sign asymmetry between UPWARD and DOWNWARD), and whether the perturbed signatures still verify and still satisfy ||s||^2 <= beta^2. Separately instrument the Julia implementation to count how many of the ~1e7 intermediate values per signature ever fall in the subnormal range, to decide whether FTZ/DAZ can matter at all. Finish by running scripts/key_recovery.jl on one directed-rounding discrepant pair to confirm it is a key-recovery pair and not merely a different signature.
- kill: Dead if directed rounding gives a rate indistinguishable from the RNE spelling arms (~1e-5 rather than ~1e-4), which would refute the systematic-sign model. Dead as an *attack* if directed-rounding signatures fail verification at a noticeable rate (then it is denial of service, which the caller would notice immediately, not a silent oracle). The FTZ half is dead if zero subnormals are ever observed -- in which case report that as the normative simplification it is.

## 35. Exact integer centres at the last six calls: the countermeasure section 7.1 could not deploy
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: The project showed 2024/1709's countermeasure is undeployable because part 2 (odd ||(g,-f)||^2) is impossible on the reference key generator, leaving part 1 to immunise only the harmless end. ## 36. A precision floor for interoperability, and the regime change below it
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: There is a mantissa precision p* at which the divergence rate stops being dominated by the four structured positions (rate ~ 2/q + 2/||(g,-f)||^2, precision-independent) and becomes dominated by all 2n generic ones (rate ~ 4n * 2^-p * |mu|, with |mu| ~ 300). Setting them equal gives p* ~ 32 bits, so a float32 or 32-bit fixed-point FALCON -- the regime of the FPGA and Cortex-M implementations -- disagrees with a double implementation on roughly 1 signature in 30, with divergences spread over the whole traversal instead of the four ends. That is a *number* FIPS 206 needs and does not have: the specification's 2^-46 bound is about the sampled distribution, and no one has derived the precision required for cross-implementation determinism. This is distinct from the established uniform-epsilon-at-every-centre law, which perturbs only centres; here every FFT and tree intermediate is truncated, which is what a reduced-precision implementation actually does.
- experiment: Add a round-to-p-significand-bits hook after every FP primitive in fft.jl and ffsampling.jl (a 3-line bit-mask on the Float64 payload), and measure the divergence rate against the untruncated baseline at p in {53,45,40,36,32,30,28,24}. Because the rate is large at small p, 1e3-1e4 signatures per point suffice at p <= 32 and the expensive points are only p >= 40. At each p record the histogram of the *first* divergent sampler call and fit rate(p) = A + B*4n*2^-p to locate the crossover; also check whether the sampled z distribution stays within the specification's Renyi bound at each p, so the determinism floor can be reported next to the security floor.
- kill: Dead if the position histogram of the first divergent call stays concentrated on calls 1,2 and 2n-1,2n even at p = 24 -- that would mean the generic background never takes over and there is no regime change, only a rate increase. Also dead if the fitted A (the precision-independent floor) is not consistent with the independently measured 2/q + 2/||(g,-f)||^2.

## 37. Key recovery from divergences that are not at the last two calls
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: The merge tower maps one leaf's two sampled values to a 2-sparse polynomial x^s(a + b x^{n/2}): merging f0 upward gives a + b x^{2^k} and a merge as f1 multiplies by x, so *every* leaf, not only the last, has 2-sparse support with a known shift s. So a divergence whose only affected leaf is any leaf of the z0 descent gives ds = x^s (a + b x^{n/2}) (g,-f) and 2024/1709 section 5.1 recovers the key with only an extra known shift. The real obstruction is not the position but the PRNG desynchronisation, which makes every later leaf differ and dz0 dense-but-short; we conjecture recovery still works for a divergence at the k-th-from-last leaf while the (2k)-sparse search stays feasible (k=2: 39^4 = 2.3e6; k=3: 39^6 = 3.5e9 with the shift structure known and prunable). Combined with the reduced-precision regime change, this decides whether a low-precision implementation leaks keys or only short vectors.
- experiment: Add a hook that injects a 1-ulp perturbation at a *chosen* leaf only, forcing a divergence at a controlled position; harvest pairs for the last leaf, the last-but-one, the last-but-two, and a mid-descent leaf, plus one first-half (z1) leaf as the negative control. For each, extend scripts/key_recovery.jl to search over the 2k-sparse dz0 with the known shifts and report success and search cost. For the dense mid-descent case, instead build the Z-module of solutions of ds0*f + ds1*g = 0 (rank n, containing (f,g)) and try LLL on it, reporting honestly whether its shortest-vector instance is as hard as NTRU.
- kill: Dead beyond the last leaf if recovery fails for the last-but-one leaf, i.e. the PRNG desync always makes dz0 dense; dead entirely for interior positions if the solution module's shortest-vector problem has lambda_1/covol^(1/n) near 1 (no exploitable gap) as a rough Gaussian-heuristic estimate suggests it might. Also dead if the 2-sparse claim is wrong -- check first, cheaply, by flipping one leaf and counting the nonzero coefficients of dz0.

## 38. Predict the leak rate without Monte Carlo: an automatic discontinuity analyser
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: Every measurement in this project so far has cost 1e5-1e6 signatures per arm. The rate is in fact computable from a single forward-mode automatic-differentiation pass: for each discontinuous predicate P in the implementation (floor(mu) in SamplerZ, BerExp's byte comparison, the gaussian0 CDT comparison) and each candidate perturbation direction, dual numbers give the sensitivity d(argument of P)/d(perturbation) exactly, and rate = sum over calls of Pr[dist-to-discontinuity < |sensitivity| * ulp], where the distance distribution is measured once. This turns 'which respellings reach the signature' from a sampling question into a computation, and it is a general tool: point it at any floating-point cryptographic implementation, get back the map of which operations can reach a discontinuity and at what rate. Ground truth to validate against already exists (A1 = 1.9e-5 over 525000, A2 = 7.5e-6 over 400000, B = 0, first-two |dmu| exactly 0 for A2).
- experiment: Write a ~60-line dual-number Float64 wrapper (value + perturbation derivative, no packages needed) and run the whole signing path once per message with the perturbation seeded at the operation of interest; record, per sampler call, both the accumulated sensitivity and the distance from mu to the nearest integer. Predict the rate for A1, A2, A2a, A2b and B and compare against the five measured arms. Then use it in *forward* mode across all FP primitives (div_fft, mul_fft, add_fft, sub_fft, split_fft, merge_fft, the leaf normalisation) to produce the map of which primitives can reach a centre at all, and confirm the tool independently predicts zero for arm B and zero for the A2 first-two positions. Generality check: run it against a Mitaka-style continuous-centre sampler and confirm it predicts zero for structural reasons.
- kill: Dead if the predicted rates miss the measured A1 and A2 rates by more than a factor of ~2, or if it predicts a nonzero first-two rate for A2 where the direct measurement gives exactly zero. Dead as a *general* tool if the prediction requires per-scheme hand analysis of the distance distribution rather than measuring it automatically in the same pass.

## 43. Adversarial spelling search: a KAT-passing, spec-defensible, key-leaking implementation
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days

## 45. Exact-arithmetic hardening of the four sensitive centres: the countermeasure §7.1 cannot deploy
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: 2024/1709 §7.1's fix has two halves; the paper shows the deployable half (round instead of floor) immunises only the harmless positions, because the reference key generator forces ‖(g,−f)‖² even, and the undeployable half (odd ‖(g,−f)‖²) is what would fix the key-recovery positions. ## 46. A certified bound: interval propagation under an implementation-freedom model
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: Every divergence number in this line of work is a measured Poisson rate with a wide CI. One can instead compute a *sound upper bound*: model implementation freedom as a per-operation ulp interval (a conforming implementation may return anything within k ulp of the correctly-rounded result, with k=0 for IEEE-mandated ops, k=1 for reassociable sums, k=2 for complex division, k=1 for contracted FMA), propagate that through the tree and the descent in interval arithmetic, and bound the divergence probability by P(the resulting centre interval contains an integer). The same instrument yields a matching lower bound that is *precision-independent*: at an exactly-integer centre, one implementation computes integer+δ_A and another integer+δ_B, and they disagree whenever sign(δ_A) ≠ sign(δ_B) — which no increase in precision prevents. Hence a theorem: for any two implementations with distinct rounding on the critical path, the divergence rate is Θ(1/‖(g,−f)‖²) independent of mantissa width, so precision-based fixes are provably useless and only structural fixes work.
- experiment: Implement an `Interval{Float64}` (or affine-form) number type and re-run `gram_fft`/`ffldl_fft`/`normalize_tree!`/`ffsampling_fft` generically — Julia's dispatch makes this a type substitution plus a handful of method definitions. Front-end it with a *census*: enumerate every FP→discrete decision in the whole scheme and attach its margin. The census is already known to contain more than the sampler: `floor(μ)` in `src/samplerz.jl`, the BerExp acceptance comparison, `round(Int, v0[i])` at `src/falcon.jl:263-264` (exact-integer value, error ~1e-9, so ~8 orders of headroom — a *provably safe* site), `k = round(Int64, x)` at `src/ntrugen.jl:737`, and the `gs_norm` rejection at `:875-883`. - kill: Dead if interval widths blow up through the ~10 levels of the tree (the classic dependency problem — `div_fft` divides by quantities whose intervals may span zero), giving a bound like 'divergence probability ≤ 1' that is vacuous. ## 60. What a first-end divergence actually leaks: break or justify 2024/1709 §5's dismissal

## 64. The trigger is invisible to timing and loud in power: an exact separation
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: Two complementary claims about the near-integer-centre event. (a) Timing is provably blind: E[accept] per SamplerZ round = (ccs/2S) * rho_sigma(Z - r), and by Poisson summation rho_sigma(Z-r) = sigma*sqrt(2pi)*(1 + O(exp(-2 pi^2 sigma^2))) with exp(-2 pi^2 sigma_min^2) ~ 1e-14, while ccs = sigma_min/sigma cancels the sigma. (b) A value-level probe is not blind: P(accept | b, z0) swings by +/-0.35 as r goes 0 -> 1/2 (at r=0 the b=0 and b=1 branches differ by exactly ccs/S ~ 0.353), and at an exactly integer centre the (z0=0, b=0) branch has x EXACTLY 0.0, so trunc(x*2^63) = 0 and all 12 Horner steps of approxexp become zero-operand UInt128 multiplies - the loudest possible Hamming-weight event, occurring with probability 0.18 per rejection round. An attacker with an EM/power probe therefore detects 'this signature is at the sensitive position' with O(1) traces and converts the 1e-5 blind search into a targeted one, while a timing-only attacker provably cannot.
- experiment: All parts run on samplerz alone, no full signing. (a) Verify the theta identity numerically at 400 bits, then measure the mean rejection-round count on a 1e4-point grid in r with 1e6 draws each and confirm the null holds down to the Monte-Carlo floor. (b) Instrument samplerz to record x; confirm x === 0.0 bit-exactly when r == 0 and (z0,b) = (0,0), and measure the branch probability. (c) Compute the Hamming-weight distribution of the approxexp operand chain with and without an integer centre and report the distinguisher's SNR and required trace count, comparing against what Guerreau et al. (TCHES 2022) achieved on Falcon's sampler. (d) Run scripts/dudect.jl on samplerz with fixed r=0 vs random r, with the existing positive/negative controls and noise floor, to confirm no timing signal. The real power measurement needs an oscilloscope and a target board - NOT available here; parts (a)-(d) are the software half and stand alone as the separation theorem.

## 71. The KAT-power theorem: conformance-suite size is the attacker's work factor

## 72. Witness vectors: a conformance suite with detection power 1 instead of 1e-5

## 73. No tolerance separates harmless from dangerous: the FN-DSA conformance trilemma
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: Conformance testing of a numerical algorithm normally admits a tolerance. FN-DSA admits none, and this is provable from the project's own two measurements pointing in opposite directions: the *smallest* differences (1 ulp, |dmu| ~ 1e-16, at a near-integer centre) change the whole signature and leak the key, while a *large* difference (fpr_inv_sigma, +941 ulp, changing 452 of 512 tree leaves) changes nothing observable, by Lemma 2. Therefore (i) output KATs are too weak (idea 1), (ii) any tolerance-based intermediate check has a strictly worse ROC than a coin flip in the region that matters, and (iii) bit-exact intermediate KATs are too strong -- they reject behaviourally identical implementations -- and additionally disclose key-dependent data, since the centre mu = n/||(g,-f)||^2 reveals a private-key invariant. Three horns, no sound conformance criterion.
- experiment: Build the ROC explicitly. Define a perturbation zoo at each pipeline stage (FFT output, Gram matrix, LDL D11, tree leaves, sigma, centre) parameterised by magnitude eps. For each stage and eps, measure over 10^4-10^5 signatures P[signature differs] using the existing precision_law.jl harness (which already gives rate ~ 2n*eps for injected uniform eps, and the 6-orders-of-magnitude excess for real respellings). Then for each candidate conformance rule 'accept iff |x - x_ref| <= tau at stage S', compute P[reject | behaviourally identical] (using the fpr_inv_sigma arm as the ground-truth harmless implementation) and P[accept | key-leaking] (using arms A1/A2/A3). Plot the ROC per stage; show no operating point achieves both < 0.05. Separately, quantify horn (iii): show that publishing the centres of a KAT vector reveals ||(g,-f)||^2 exactly (via the rational-structure check already in denominator_check.jl).
- kill: Dead if some intermediate value gives a clean separation -- the obvious candidate is the integer sampler output vector z (or s2 before compression): if requiring bit-exact z accepts every behaviourally identical implementation and rejects every diverging one, the trilemma collapses to 'publish z' and the problem is solved rather than impossible. Test that candidate first, because it is the one that can kill the idea in an afternoon.

## 74. A FIPS 140-3 module cannot self-test its way out: CAST power ~2e-5, PCT power exactly 0
- impact: **solid** | novelty risk: medium | cost: minutes
- hypothesis: Every self-test mechanism FIPS 140-3 gives a cryptographic module is blind to this failure. The pre-operational/conditional CAST for a signature algorithm is a single known-answer test, so its detection power equals the per-signature divergence rate, ~2e-5. The pairwise consistency test (sign-then-verify), which is the standard fallback for algorithms whose signature is not a deterministic function of its inputs -- and which is what a randomized-only FN-DSA forces a module to use -- has detection power *exactly zero*, because a divergent execution still produces a signature that passes verification: that is precisely why the attack works. Consequence: a validated FIPS 140-3 module can be key-leaking, and no self-test in the standard's catalogue can notice.
- experiment: (1) Take the divergent pairs already on disk (paper.md 6.1, 7.1; scripts/first_divergence.jl, scripts/countermeasure_break.jl outputs) and run both members of each pair through falcon_verify and through the C reference verifier; confirm both accept and both satisfy the norm bound -- PCT power 0, measured, in minutes. (2) Compute the CAST power as 1-(1-rho)^k for the k=1 and k=3 vectors a CAST typically uses. (3) Read FIPS 140-3 IG 10.3.A and the SP 800-140 series (fetchable from csrc.nist.gov via WebSearch/WebFetch, or reconstructible from the ACVP repo) for what a module is permitted to substitute for a signing KAT when the algorithm is randomized-only; write the resulting normative gap as a table. (4) State the fix a standard could mandate: a CAST run in derandomized internal mode against a *witness* vector (idea 2), which is the only self-test with power 1.
- kill: Dead if any divergent signature fails verification (then the PCT has nonzero power and the module can self-detect). Also weakened materially if FIPS 140-3 IG already requires derandomized-mode KAT self-tests for randomized signature algorithms, in which case the gap is already closed on paper and only the CAST-power number survives.

## 77. Key generation is not a function of its seed either: the orthogonalized-norm acceptance boundary
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: hours
- hypothesis: All prior work, including ePrint 2024/1709, treats this as a signing problem. It is not. Key generation contains a floating-point rejection test -- the C reference accumulates `bnorm` as an FP sum of squares in the FFT domain and rejects on `fpr_lt(bnorm, fpr_bnorm_max)` (keygen.c:4255-4269), while this project's Julia keygen computes the same quantity by an entirely different route (inverse FFT, sum |Ft|^2, times q^2; ntrugen.jl:874-887). Two conforming implementations therefore accept/reject different candidates, and since keygen is a seed-driven rejection loop, ONE flip yields a completely different key pair from the same seed. The band is not 1 ulp in the ecosystem: pornin/c-fn-dsa deliberately uses 0.999*1.17*sqrt(q) and comments 'Standard says 0.9999, we are slightly more restrictive just to [account for] fixed-point approximations' (kgen_ntru.c:1927-1931) -- a 1.8e-3 relative disagreement band with any 0.9999 implementation. If the candidate density near the bound is nonzero, seed-based key derivation, HSM key backup, and ACVP keyGen vectors all break, deterministically and visibly.
- experiment: Cheap and does not need NTRUSolve. (1) Sample ~10^6 candidate (f,g) with gen_poly and compute the orthogonalized norm at BigFloat precision; histogram bnorm/(1.17^2 q) near 1; report the mass in [0.999^2, 0.9999^2] (the c-fn-dsa vs standard band) and in a +/-1e-13 relative band (the Julia vs C-ref band). Note the l2 part of the test is integer-exact in the C reference, so restrict attention to candidates where the orthogonalized part is the max. (2) Run Julia keygen and the C reference keygen on the same seeds (scripts/cref_keygen_instr.c already instruments the C side) over as many seeds as the boundary density predicts are needed, and exhibit one seed producing two different public keys. (3) Repeat for c-fn-dsa's constant by simply evaluating both thresholds on the same candidate stream -- no need to build it.
- kill: Dead if the candidate density near the bound is negligible: if fewer than 1 in 10^4 candidates has an orthogonalized norm within 2e-3 relative of the bound, the 0.999 fudge is harmless, and if fewer than 1 in 10^8 lie within 1e-13, the two-spelling flip is unreachable. Also dead if it turns out every implementation performs this test in exact integer or exact rational arithmetic (check c-fn-dsa's fxr path, the round-3 fpr path, and PQClean before measuring).

## 80. A determinism clause FN-DSA can actually have: pinned deterministic arithmetic, with its price measured
- impact: **top-tier** | novelty risk: medium | cost: days
- hypothesis: FIPS 204 guarantees determinism with one sentence ('no floating-point arithmetic'); FIPS 205 gets it from being hash-based. The contribution is the format, the precision argument, the reference code, and the honest performance price, which this repo can already quote (FPEMU is ~6x slower than native FP at signing).
- experiment: (1) Implement ffSampling and the FFT over a fixed-point type in Julia (Int128 with a 2^-80 scale, or a double-double), reusing the existing tree/sampler structure; check the spec 2.5.2 Renyi/precision condition still holds and that signatures verify and satisfy the norm bound.
